Teacher: dataset reliability and source audit

Catalogue: 83 indicators (World Bank, OWID, UN and offline Natural Earth); 77 spatial catalogue entries (only built-in country boundaries are always offline). Availability is checked for the selected year, not assumed from a catalogue link.

Run this once as a teacher on your school network. Checking all indicators makes many external requests and is inappropriate for 22 students to launch simultaneously. Some series have no observations in specific years. A successful provider check cannot guarantee future accessibility.

2 · Statistical analysis & graphsIA / DP fieldwork
Choose a country dataset or student-imported vector layer.
Select a numerical field to calculate mean, median, sample standard deviation, quartiles and range. Select two fields for Pearson r, Spearman ρ and a regression line.

Spearman uses average tied ranks. Quartiles use linear interpolation. Exclude missing records and describe the resulting sample size. Correlation and regression do not demonstrate causation.

3 · Spatial analysisGeography IA

Use your imported fieldwork points, roads or polygons, the country boundary layer, or your drawings. Every successful operation creates a new, editable GIS vector layer.

Geographical operations use an approximate spherical earth model. Verify the suitability of distances and geometries for your investigation.

Results will appear here and in the Layers panel.

Point circles use geodesic bearings. Line buffers use approximate local planar offsets; do not interpret them as legal/setback boundaries. Polygon overlay selects whole intersecting features, not clipped geometries. Local hotspot z-scores are exploratory, without multiple-comparison or edge correction.

4 · Change over timeCore & HL
Start with a World Bank, UN SDG or Our World in Data indicator, then compare two reported years. Not all indicators have data for every year.

A time-series graph shows up to eight sampled reported years, not inferred annual values. Percent change uses the absolute starting value in the denominator; zero starting values are excluded. Check definitions and territorial comparability over time.

Sources & limitations

Vector data imports: CSV, GeoJSON, zipped shapefile (Shpjs), KML and GPX. Multi-scale tools: open geoBoundaries subdivisions, official Hong Kong district boundaries, optional OpenStreetMap points, and uploaded georeferenced map images. Image georeferencing supports WGS84 bounds, not automatic transformation. Custom XYZ/WMS endpoints must be permitted by their providers. Country outlines and historical estimates: Natural Earth (via public GeoPandas sample dataset). Live indicators: World Bank Development Indicators API. Street basemap: OpenFreeMap © OpenStreetMap contributors; terrain: OpenTopoMap; imagery: Esri. Country boundaries are low resolution and some territories are omitted. Street maps use OpenFreeMap vector styles (no API key); local file and school network access still require checking. This is an educational tool, not a replacement for professional GIS software.
